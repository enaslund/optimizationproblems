# Linnik's constant

## Description of constant

For integers $q\ge 2$ and $a$ with $\gcd(a,q)=1$, let $P(a,q)$ denote the least prime in the arithmetic progression $a \bmod q$.
<a href="#Xyl2011-def-Paq">[Xyl2011-def-Paq]</a>

Linnik's theorem asserts that there exist constants $C,L>0$ such that

$$
P(a,q)\ \le\ C\,q^{L}
\qquad
(\gcd(a,q)=1),
$$

uniformly for all $q\ge 2$.
<a href="#Xyl2011-def-Linnik">[Xyl2011-def-Linnik]</a>

We define

$$
C_{65}\ :=\ L_{\mathrm{Lin}},
$$

where $L_{\mathrm{Lin}}$ is the infimum of all exponents $L$ for which such a bound holds (with some constant $C$ independent of $a,q$).
<a href="#Xyl2011-def-Linnik">[Xyl2011-def-Linnik]</a>

The best known exponent reported in a modern source is

$$
L_{\mathrm{Lin}}\ \le\ 5.
$$

<a href="#MMT2024-ub-5">[MMT2024-ub-5]</a>

A 2026 computer-assisted preprint proves $L_{\mathrm{Lin}}\le 3.99$; the table of upper bounds below records it, together with its verification status.
<a href="#Nas2026-ub-3-99">[Nas2026-ub-3-99]</a>

A trivial lower bound is $L_{\mathrm{Lin}}\ge 1$, since for $a=1$ the first candidate prime is at least $q+1$.

## Additional comments and links

- **Terminology.** Some sources call $L_{\mathrm{Lin}}$ "Linnik's constant" and reserve "Linnik's theorem" for the existence of some finite exponent $L$.
  <a href="#Xyl2011-def-Linnik">[Xyl2011-def-Linnik]</a>

- **Methodological progress after classical zero-density proofs.** A recent result gives a new $L$-function-free proof with bound $p\ll q^{350}$.
  <a href="#MMT2024-ub-350">[MMT2024-ub-350]</a>

- **Verification status of the $3.99$ bound.** The proof of [[Nas2026](#Nas2026)] is computer-assisted and has not yet been refereed.
  - [[Pal2026](#Pal2026)] registers a Lean 4 formalization, using only the standard axioms, of two components of the proof. The first is the graded near-density lemma (Theorem 8.4 of the paper) with its zero form, conditional on four published estimates stated as explicit hypotheses: Xylouris's Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2, Burgess's character-sum bound with $k=3$, and Graham's estimate for Selberg-sieve sums. The second is the soundness of the exact checker for the certificates of the leaf linear programs (Section 10 of the paper).
  - The zero-location arguments, the coverage of the case tree, the exterior ranges and the final assembly have conventional proofs and computer checks only, so the bound $L_{\mathrm{Lin}}\le 3.99$ itself is not formally verified.
  - According to the paper, the certificates are checked by three separate programs, one of them formally verified. The certificates, the programs that regenerate every leaf program and check every certificate, the independent checker, the audits and the reports of every run are public in [[NasRepo2026](#NasRepo2026)] (directory `computations/`, with instructions for rerunning each check), together with the paper and the Lean formalization. At [commit `0400629`](https://github.com/enaslund/linniks-constant-3.99/tree/0400629d89408c41c545f1fa8b7265051aa079c7) the three certificate files have SHA-256 `539362a0…fe63c` (inside), `b715ac73…b6cfc` (nodes) and `a53d4d57…5ac1d` (outside); a run from a fresh copy of that commit reproduced the full replay and the independent checker on the whole corpus.
  - The paper states that it was written, and the research carried out, by AI models (Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT Astra 6) under the author's prompting and supervision.

- **Historical progression list.** A compiled table of earlier admissible values appears in Xylouris's table of improvements.
  <a href="#Xyl2011-historical-table">[Xyl2011-historical-table]</a>

- [Wikipedia page on Linnik's theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linnik%27s_theorem)
